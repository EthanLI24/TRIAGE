## Final-checkpoint evaluation protocol

Benchmark scores are measured independently from the training sampler using a
SGLang service. AIME-2024, AIME-2025, MATH500, AMC23, and GSM8K are each
evaluated with six sampled responses per problem, decoded at temperature 0.7,
top-p 1.0, top-k −1, min-p 0, with thinking enabled and a maximum output length
of 20,480 tokens. The primary metric is mean sample accuracy, i.e.
sample-averaged pass@1 under repeated stochastic decoding. Runs that collapsed
before the planned horizon are evaluated at the last checkpoint saved before
collapse and are marked with †.

## Natural-generation RL protocol

The natural-generation table measures Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base on one node with
8 B300 GPUs, Slime with colocated Megatron/SGLang, TP/PP/CP=1, EP=8, ETP=1.
Sixteen responses are sampled per prompt, so global batch 256/512 means 16/32
prompts. Each run starts from the base checkpoint on DAPO-Math-17K with seed 42,
the same input order, natural EOS, and a 20,480-token completion limit. Two
iterations are warm-up; ten iterations (2–11) are averaged. Initialization,
evaluation, and checkpoint saving are excluded. BF16 uses the Triton sampler
backend; R3 capture and reference-policy KL evaluation are disabled.

Rollout throughput is the arithmetic mean of each iteration's **actual generated
completion tokens / rollout seconds**, not total tokens divided by total time.
Iteration wall time includes rollout, learner update, weight conversion and
synchronization, and orchestration. Natural output lengths vary across precision
settings, so this is not a fixed-output kernel-speed comparison.

## Paired learner overhead

One frozen batch on 8 B300 GPUs, five paired repetitions, gating and Repair off
versus on. The median overhead is **0.84%** and the median absolute increase is
**0.225 s**. This is learner-update overhead, not a percentage of rollout time,
loss-kernel time, or end-to-end wall time.

## Standalone sampler after configuration selection

Qwen3-30B-A3B, one B300 GPU, 512 responses × 4,096 tokens, client concurrency
512, BF16 KV cache. Both precisions receive a twelve-candidate configuration
search, top-two remeasurement, and five final measurements on the same GPU.
Both selected configurations use the FlashInfer TRT-LLM MoE backend. The
different selected memory fractions are part of equal-opportunity tuning, not a
matched-memory test.

| Precision | Memory fraction | Median tokens/s | Min–max tokens/s | Repeats |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BF16 | 0.95 | 15,470.01 | 15,447.56–15,509.83 | 5 |
| NVFP4 | 0.90 | 18,175.67 | 18,126.37–18,195.69 | 5 |

The ratio is **1.175×** (17.5% higher sampler throughput). It is distinct from
the 2.35× natural-generation rollout result above (the NVFP4 batch-512 row):
fixed output length, one GPU, and no learner. Runtime for these measurements:
PyTorch 2.9.1+cu130, SGLang 0.5.10, FlashInfer 0.6.12, and sglang-kernel
0.4.1+cu130.

## Controlled long-tail workloads

Synthetic long-tail batches served by eight independent single-GPU replicas.
For N=256, response lengths are 224×2,048, 24×8,192, and 8×20,480 tokens; N=512
doubles each count. The request-to-length mapping is fixed with seed 20260912.
A uniform 3,200-token control has the same total output count.

| Responses | BF16 median s | NVFP4 median s | Time ratio | Uniform-control ratio | Repeats |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 256 | 156.13 | 120.83 | 1.292× | 1.538× | 5 |
| 512 | 165.26 | 129.28 | 1.278× | 1.513× | 3 |

The mixed-length batch finishes about 35–36 seconds sooner under NVFP4, but its
relative speedup is smaller than the equal-token uniform control. These
measurements do not establish that longer tails amplify the speedup.

These sampler/reward harness measurements exclude learner updates and weight

## Known counterexamples

NVFP4 is not uniformly faster in these measurements. Two results bound the
headline claims and are kept here deliberately:

- **30B reward-harness slowdown at N=512.** All three measured batches are
  slower under NVFP4 (median paired TPS ratio 0.665, wall-time ratio 0.631).
  The harness includes reward evaluation but excludes learner updates, so this
  result must not be generalized to RL iteration time either; it does show that
  the rollout-side speedup does not automatically propagate through every
  pipeline stage.
- **4B fixed-output near-parity.** Across the 4B concurrency and length scans,
  NVFP4 serving throughput stays within 1.004–1.026× of BF16. The 30B sampler
  speedups (up to 1.55×) should not be extrapolated to dense 4B serving.
